Privacy

Cairn is a small personal project, used by its author and a few friends. This page describes exactly what the app touches and where it goes, in plain language.

The short version

  • Your notes and reminders are stored in Cairn's Google Cloud Firestore database, where only your account can read them.
  • Images and recordings are stored in your own Google Drive, not in Cairn's database.
  • Your email is read on your own device and is never sent to any server the developer runs.
  • So your phone and computer show the same inbox, a short summary of each new email and Classroom post — never its full text — is kept in the same database, readable only by your account.
  • Only if you add your own NVIDIA API key, the text of new emails and Classroom posts is sent to NVIDIA's AI service to be sorted. The key is kept in your account in the database, so your phone and computer both use it. Without a key, nothing leaves your device for this.
  • The developer runs no server of their own. Nothing is sold, shared or analysed.

What Cairn reads, and why

Your name, email address and profile picture
To sign you in and to label which account is connected.
Gmail (read-only)
To find deadlines — assignment due dates in Google Classroom notification emails, and dates mentioned by teachers, and to pick out mail that matters. Message content is read on your device. Only the result is saved, as described under The shared inbox. Message bodies are never stored and never transmitted to the developer. They go to an AI service only as described under Automatic sorting of email.
Google Classroom (read-only)
To read your course list, assignments and their due dates, announcements, and whether you have submitted an assignment — so new work and announcements show in Cairn's Classroom tab, and a reminder made from an assignment can tick itself off when you hand the work in. If you turn on “Automatically add tasks”, new assignments and announcements become reminders without you tapping anything.
Google Drive (files created by Cairn only)
To store images and audio you attach to a note. Cairn uses the drive.file scope, which means it can only see files it created itself. It cannot read the rest of your Drive.
Google Drive, read-only, and Classroom materials (only if you turn on Classroom files)
In the Windows app you can choose to save the files your teachers attach in Google Classroom. Those files belong to your teachers, not to Cairn, so reading them needs read-only access to Drive files shared with you (drive.readonly), and classroom.courseworkmaterials.readonly to see posts that are only a material. Cairn opens only the files attached to posts in the classes you pick, and saves them to the folder you choose on your computer. They are not uploaded, synced or sent anywhere else, and Cairn never changes or deletes anything in your Drive or in that folder. Turning the setting off stops it; you can remove the access at any time as described below.

Where your data lives

Notes, reminders and their metadata are stored in Cloud Firestore, in the Google Cloud project that runs Cairn. Access is restricted by security rules so that a signed-in account can read and write only its own data; no other user can see it. As the owner of that project, the developer could technically view the database through Google's console, and does not. Attachments are not in the database at all: they are stored in the signed-in user's own Google Drive and count against that account's storage. The full text of an email is never stored in the database.

If you connect more than one Google account — for example a personal account and a university account — only the first is used to store data. Any additional account is used solely to read its own Gmail and Classroom, and nothing is written to it.

A note you delete goes to Cairn's recycle bin for 30 days, where you can restore it. After 30 days it is deleted for good from your phone, your computer and the database. Its images and recordings are moved to your Drive's trash when the note is deleted, and Google empties Drive's trash after 30 days.

The shared inbox

Only one of your devices reads Gmail and Classroom at a time — your computer while Cairn is running there, otherwise your phone — and it saves what it found in the database so the other shows the same inbox. For each new email or Classroom post that is: the sender (or course) and subject, Gmail's own short preview of at most 200 characters, which connected account it arrived in, when, Gmail's message and conversation ids, and what was made of it — whether it is important, its folder, tags, a one-line summary, any deadline and suggested task — plus whether you have opened, hidden or made a task of it. The full text and attachments are never stored. The database keeps about the newest 250 of these; older ones are deleted.

The same applies to a few small settings: which Google accounts each device has connected and which services each was allowed (Gmail, Classroom, Classroom files — never a password or Google token), which device is currently reading and where it got to, your rules for senders, what you wrote under “What matters to you”, and whether Classroom posts become tasks automatically. All of it is restricted, like your notes, to your own account.

Automatic sorting of email and Classroom

Without an AI key, Cairn sorts mail on your device with fixed rules, and no third party is involved.

If, and only if, you add your own NVIDIA API key in Settings → Inbox & AI, each new email, and each new Classroom assignment and announcement, is sent to NVIDIA's AI service (integrate.api.nvidia.com) to decide whether it is important, which folder and tags it belongs under, a one-line summary, and any deadline or time in it. What is sent is the sender (or the course name), the subject or title, when it arrived, any Classroom due date, up to the first 6,000 characters of the text, and what you wrote under “What matters to you” in Settings. Attachments are never sent. While a key is set, Cairn also reads the Promotions and Social tabs so that nothing important is missed there. Spam and Trash are never read.

The request is made directly from whichever of your devices is reading your mail, with your key. It does not pass through any server the developer runs. So that both devices can sort mail, the key you paste is saved in your own account in Cairn's Firestore database, where security rules let only your signed-in account read it, and in each device's secure storage. It is never shared with anyone else, and it is sent only to NVIDIA. What NVIDIA does with a request is governed by NVIDIA's terms for the key you created, so read them before adding one. Removing the key in Settings deletes it from the database and from your devices and turns AI sorting off straight away.

When you mark a sender as important or not important, that choice is saved as a rule in your account, so both devices follow it. It takes priority over the AI, and it is never sent to NVIDIA or anyone else. Cairn does not use your data to train any AI model, and does not ask anyone else to.

What is not collected

  • No analytics, telemetry or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, and no sharing or selling of data to anyone.
  • No account on any service operated by the developer, because there isn't one.

Removing access

You can disconnect any account from inside Cairn, which deletes the stored credentials for it. You can also revoke Cairn's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which works whether or not the app is installed. Deleting the Firestore database removes stored notes, reminders, inbox summaries, settings and your AI key; deleting the files from your Drive removes attachments.

Limited Use

Cairn's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
