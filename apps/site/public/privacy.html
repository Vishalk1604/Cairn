Privacy

Cairn is a small personal project, used by its author and a few friends. This page describes exactly what the app touches and where it goes, in plain language.

The short version

  • Your notes and reminders are stored in Cairn's Google Cloud Firestore database, where only your account can read them.
  • Images and recordings are stored in your own Google Drive, not in Cairn's database.
  • Your email is read on your own device and is never sent to any server the developer runs.
  • Only if you add your own NVIDIA API key, the text of new emails is sent to NVIDIA's AI service to be sorted. Without a key, nothing leaves your device for this.
  • The developer runs no server of their own. Nothing is sold, shared or analysed.

What Cairn reads, and why

Your name, email address and profile picture
To sign you in and to label which account is connected.
Gmail (read-only)
To find deadlines — assignment due dates in Google Classroom notification emails, and dates mentioned by teachers, and to pick out mail that matters. Message content is read on your device. Only the result — such as a one-line summary, a folder name or “a reminder due Friday” — is saved, on your phone. Message bodies are never stored and never transmitted to the developer. They go to an AI service only as described under Automatic sorting of email.
Google Classroom (read-only)
To read your course list, assignments and their due dates, announcements, and whether you have submitted an assignment — so a reminder can tick itself off when you hand work in.
Google Drive (files created by Cairn only)
To store images and audio you attach to a note. Cairn uses the drive.file scope, which means it can only see files it created itself. It cannot read the rest of your Drive.

Where your data lives

Notes, reminders and their metadata are stored in Cloud Firestore, in the Google Cloud project that runs Cairn. Access is restricted by security rules so that a signed-in account can read and write only its own data; no other user can see it. As the owner of that project, the developer could technically view the database through Google's console, and does not. Attachments are not in the database at all: they are stored in the signed-in user's own Google Drive and count against that account's storage. Email is never stored in the database.

If you connect more than one Google account — for example a personal account and a university account — only the first is used to store data. Any additional account is used solely to read its own Gmail and Classroom, and nothing is written to it.

Automatic sorting of email

Without an AI key, Cairn sorts mail on your device with fixed rules, and no third party is involved.

If, and only if, you add your own NVIDIA API key in Settings → AI sorting, each new email is sent to NVIDIA's AI service (integrate.api.nvidia.com) to decide whether it is important, which folder it belongs in, a one-line summary, and any deadline in it. What is sent is the sender, the subject, when it arrived, and up to the first 6,000 characters of the message text. Attachments are never sent. While a key is set, Cairn also reads the Promotions and Social tabs so that nothing important is missed there. Spam and Trash are never read.

The request is made directly from your phone, with your key. It does not pass through any server the developer runs. Your key is kept in your phone's secure storage and is never synced or shared. What NVIDIA does with a request is governed by NVIDIA's terms for the key you created, so read them before adding one. Removing the key in Settings turns AI sorting off straight away.

When you mark a sender as important or not important, that choice is saved on your phone as a rule. It takes priority over the AI, and it is never sent anywhere. Cairn does not use your data to train any AI model, and does not ask anyone else to.

What is not collected

  • No analytics, telemetry or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, and no sharing or selling of data to anyone.
  • No account on any service operated by the developer, because there isn't one.

Removing access

You can disconnect any account from inside Cairn, which deletes the stored credentials for it. You can also revoke Cairn's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which works whether or not the app is installed. Deleting the Firestore database removes stored notes and reminders; deleting the files from your Drive removes attachments.

Limited Use

Cairn's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
