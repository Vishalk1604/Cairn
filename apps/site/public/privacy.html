Privacy policy

Privacy

Last updated 28 October 2026

Cairn is a small project, built by Vishal Karun and open to students during its beta. This page describes exactly what the app touches and where it goes, in plain language.

The short version

  • Your notes and reminders are stored in Cairn's Google Cloud Firestore database, where only your account can read them.
  • Images and recordings are stored in your own Google Drive, not in Cairn's database.
  • If you connect Gmail or Google Classroom, Cairn's server — a small program on Cloudflare Workers — reads them for you every 10 minutes, so new coursework and important mail reach your phone even while it is off. It keeps your Google permission and, if you add one, your NVIDIA AI key, both encrypted. It does not keep your email.
  • What it found is saved as a short summary of each new email and Classroom post — never its full text — in the same database as your notes, readable only by your account, so your phone and computer show the same inbox.
  • Only if you add your own NVIDIA API key, the text of new emails and Classroom posts is sent to NVIDIA's AI service to be sorted.
  • Notifications reach your phone through Firebase Cloud Messaging.
  • Nothing is sold, shared or analysed.

What Cairn reads, and why

Your name, email address and profile picture
To sign you in and to label which account is connected.
Gmail (read-only)
To pick out mail that matters and the deadlines in it. Cairn's server reads new mail in your inbox as it arrives and saves only the result, as described under The shared inbox. Message bodies are never stored in the database and never kept by the server, except briefly, encrypted, while a message waits to be sorted (see Cairn's server). They go to an AI service only as described under Automatic sorting. Spam and Trash are never read.
Google Classroom (read-only)
To read your course list, assignments and their due dates, announcements, and whether you have submitted an assignment — so new work and announcements show in Cairn's Classroom tab, and a reminder made from an assignment can tick itself off when you hand the work in. If you turn on “Automatically add tasks”, new assignments and announcements become reminders without you tapping anything.
Google Drive (files created by Cairn only)
To store images and audio you attach to a note. Cairn uses the drive.file scope, which means it can only see files it created itself. It cannot read the rest of your Drive. Your phone uploads and your computer downloads these files directly; they do not pass through Cairn's server.
Google Drive, read-only, and Classroom materials (only if you turn on Classroom files)
In the Windows app you can choose to save the files your teachers attach in Google Classroom. Those files belong to your teachers, not to Cairn, so reading them needs read-only access to Drive files shared with you (drive.readonly), and classroom.courseworkmaterials.readonly to see posts that are only a material. Your computer downloads only the files attached to posts in the classes you pick, straight from Google, and saves them to the folder you choose. They are not uploaded, synced or sent anywhere else, and Cairn never changes or deletes anything in your Drive or in that folder.

Cairn's server

Cairn's server is a program run by the developer on Cloudflare Workers, Cloudflare's hosting for small programs. When you sign in or connect an account, Google gives the permission you granted to this server, and it is the only place it is kept; your phone and computer hold only a sign-in to your Cairn account. With it, the server checks every 10 minutes for new mail and Classroom posts in the accounts and services you chose, writes what it found to your account in the database, and sends your phone a notification when something needs you, plus a summary of what is due in the evening and in the morning. When your computer needs a file from Drive, it asks the server for a short-lived permission for that alone.

The server keeps, in Cloudflare's database: your Google permissions, your NVIDIA key if you added one, your phone's notification address and time zone, where it got to in each mailbox, and the text of any message still waiting to be sorted (for at most three days). Everything sensitive is encrypted with a key only the server holds. It keeps no copy of your notes, reminders or inbox, and no log of your mail.

It writes to the database as you, so the same rules apply to it as to your own devices: it can reach only your account's data. As the person who runs it, the developer could technically change the program, and does not look at anyone's data.

Where your data lives

Notes, reminders and their metadata are stored in Cloud Firestore, in the Google Cloud project that runs Cairn. Access is restricted by security rules so that a signed-in account can read and write only its own data; no other user can see it. As the owner of that project, the developer could technically view the database through Google's console, and does not. Attachments are not in the database at all: they are stored in the signed-in user's own Google Drive and count against that account's storage. The full text of an email is never stored in the database.

If you connect more than one Google account — for example a personal account and a university account — only the first is used to store data. Any additional account is used solely to read its own Gmail and Classroom, and nothing is written to it.

A note you delete goes to Cairn's recycle bin for 30 days, where you can restore it. After 30 days it is deleted for good from your phone, your computer and the database. Its images and recordings are moved to your Drive's trash when the note is deleted, and Google empties Drive's trash after 30 days.

The shared inbox

For each new email or Classroom post, Cairn's server saves in the database: the sender (or course) and subject, Gmail's own short preview of at most 200 characters, which connected account it arrived in, when, Gmail's message and conversation ids, and what was made of it — whether it is important, its folder, tags, a one-line summary, any deadline and suggested task — plus whether you have opened, hidden or made a task of it. The full text and attachments are never stored. The database keeps about the newest 250 of these; older ones are deleted.

A few small settings are kept there too: which Google accounts are connected and which services each was allowed (never a password, token or key), when the server last checked, your rules for senders, what you wrote under “What matters to you”, your time zone, and whether Classroom posts become tasks automatically. All of it is restricted, like your notes, to your own account.

Automatic sorting of email and Classroom

Without an AI key, Cairn's server sorts mail with fixed rules, and no third party is involved.

If, and only if, you add your own NVIDIA API key on your phone, each new email, and each new Classroom assignment and announcement, is sent by Cairn's server to NVIDIA's AI service (integrate.api.nvidia.com) to decide whether it is important, which folder and tags it belongs under, a one-line summary, and any deadline or time in it. What is sent is the sender (or the course name), the subject or title, when it arrived, any Classroom due date, up to the first 6,000 characters of the text, and what you wrote under “What matters to you”. Attachments are never sent. While a key is set, Cairn also reads the Promotions and Social tabs so that nothing important is missed there.

The key you paste is kept, encrypted, on Cairn's server, and in your phone's secure storage. It is never stored in the database, never shared with anyone else, and sent only to NVIDIA. What NVIDIA does with a request is governed by NVIDIA's terms for the key you created, so read them before adding one. Removing the key on your phone deletes it from the server and the phone and turns AI sorting off straight away.

When you mark a sender as important or not important, that choice is saved as a rule in your account, so both devices and the server follow it. It takes priority over the AI, and it is never sent to NVIDIA or anyone else. Cairn does not use your data to train any AI model, and does not ask anyone else to.

Notifications

To notify your phone when the app is closed, Cairn's server sends notifications through Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google's notification service. A notification carries a title and a line of text — the course and the title of new coursework, or the summary of an important email — and which item to open. Your phone's notification address is kept on the server and forgotten when you sign out.

What is not collected

  • No analytics, telemetry or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, and no sharing or selling of data to anyone.
  • No copy of your email, notes or reminders on Cairn's server.

Removing access

You can disconnect any additional account on your phone, which deletes Cairn's server's copy of its permission and gives it back to Google. “Stop and forget everything” in your phone's settings stops every check and deletes everything the server keeps for you. You can also revoke Cairn's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which works whether or not the app is installed; the server then can no longer read anything. Deleting the Firestore database removes stored notes, reminders, inbox summaries and settings; deleting the files from your Drive removes attachments.

Limited Use

Cairn's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
